1.5.2 — November 20, 2026

  • RTX Video HDR for SDR films. On an NVIDIA RTX card on High quality, with RTX Video HDR on in Exo Launcher's PC › Display (or NVIDIA's settings), an SDR film or show is turned into HDR by the card, in the same video processor as RTX Video Super Resolution. It runs on a screen in HDR; a screen that can show HDR and has it off is switched for the title by Auto HDR's rules (before the first frame, never during a game, back off after). Not under ArtCNN, not while a game runs, and it steps aside with the upscaler when a video keeps dropping frames. I shows RTX Video HDR. AMD and Intel have no SDR-to-HDR for video a player can call, so nothing changes there.
  • Smooth motion, in Settings › Playback. Off unless you turn it on. Each frame is shown for exactly its share of the screen's refreshes and the change to the next frame is blended over one refresh (mpv's display-resample with oversample interpolation), so a 24 fps pan no longer judders on a 60 Hz screen. It makes no new frames, so films keep their look. With sound passed through to a receiver it keeps time by repeating a frame now and then instead. Off on battery and while a game runs, and for the rest of a video that keeps dropping frames once the picture has gone lighter. I shows it as Motion, and the choice syncs with your other settings. True frame generation is not in it: NVIDIA's Smooth Motion and AMD's Fluid Motion Frames are game features with no call for a player, NVIDIA's optical-flow interpolator can't be shipped under its SDK licence, and RIFE needs PyTorch or a GPL runtime and several gigabytes beside the app.

1.5.1 — November 13, 2026

  • Updates come from Exo's update address and nowhere else. Exo Cinema no longer asks GitHub for the newest version or for a release's files when the update address doesn't answer: no answer now means no update that time. GitHub's three hosts are gone from the list of places Exo Cinema may connect to. Source and Licenses in Settings › About open getexo.app.
  • A test copy sends no reports. A copy of Exo Cinema running on its own data folder, as the tests and trial runs do, keeps its crash and freeze reports on its own disk. One such trial run reported a freeze as if a person had met it.
  • The first update that downloads only what changed. From 1.5.0, this release arrives as the handful of files that differ, not as the whole Setup. _The first real one: what the app's log said on a real install is in the open items, not here._
  • PRIVACY, README, SECURITY and CONTRIBUTING say the above; the repository's GitHub issue forms and workflows are removed.

1.5.0 — November 12, 2026

Exo Cinema plays from your Jellyfin server, and nothing else.

  • Jellyfin only. Home, Movies, Shows, search, a title's versions and playback are your server's. The other sources are gone: Settings › Add-ons, their rows and search, their stream list, the built-in public catalog, and the fallback that asked one from your PC. Until you sign in to a server, every room is the Jellyfin sign-in, and the first run ends on it.
  • History is your server's. What you played on the server, by day, the last played first.
  • What Exo Cinema kept before 1.5 is still here. Exo Watch's Watchlist and History from when it played from other sources still show in those rooms beside the server's, each title by its name; press one and it is looked up on your server. Nothing in the file or in your account is deleted, and nothing is added to it any more; where it left a title is kept but no longer shown. The list of sources a PC and an account held is left as it is on both, neither read nor sent nor deleted.
  • Fewer places it connects. The public catalog, its picture hosts and the ID lookup are no longer contacted, by anything. Pictures come only from the server you signed in to. _Tests: EgressTests, JellyfinOnlyTests (every web client has one purpose; the library's are the server's)._
  • Fixed: a request refused at every start. With titles kept in Exo Watch, 1.4.12 asked the public catalog about each one as it opened; the catalog sent a show asked as a film on to a second address Exo Cinema doesn't connect to, so the log filled with "refused a metadata request". The request is gone.
  • Fixed: Exo Cinema could freeze for good when a film's host went quiet. The player's reader waited on the connection with nothing to end the wait, so Stop, the next film and closing the window all waited with it. A read now ends when it is cancelled, and a host that sends nothing for 30 seconds is asked again from where it stopped. The seek bar's pictures had the same wait. _Found in a real run (the window stopped answering; the dump showed where). Tests: PlayStreamTests, against a host on this PC that goes quiet mid-file. The real host did not go quiet again in the runs after the fix._
  • Fixed: a film started in the first minute froze the window for five seconds. Exo Cinema builds its anime upscaler once, in the background; starting a film meanwhile waited for it on the window's own thread.
  • Fixed: a new account's recovery code wasn't shown in the first run. Creating your account there went straight to "Signed in"; the code, which is shown only once, never appeared. It is shown now, and Continue waits until you have typed it back.
  • Settings, the account and the sign-in are drawn as Exo Launcher's. Settings is a solid black sheet with one hairline (the room's art no longer shows through it), its open page marked by a white bar; the current room, season or library is a white pill; forms are one narrow column with their button on the left; the notes under most rows are gone, kept only where something is lost (Change password, New recovery code, Delete account, the recovery code). The Jellyfin sign-in is the same form in the first run, in Settings and in the rooms.
  • Help and Privacy open Exo's own pages. Settings › About's Report a problem opens getexo.app/help, and Privacy opens getexo.app/privacy.
  • The old mailbox sync's code is removed. It was switched off in 1.4.10. The one-time import of what a PC held into your account is unchanged. _Tests: EgressTests, AccountSyncTests._
  • The tests no longer write into your real Exo Cinema folder. Running them started the installed app's log afresh each time.

1.4.12 — November 12, 2026

The Exo account comes to Exo Cinema, on top of 1.4.11's fixes. (These changes were built as 1.4.8 to 1.4.10 on a line that was never published; the published 1.4.8 to 1.4.11 are the fixes below.)

  • Glass or Solid and Reduce motion follow you to every PC. A choice made on one PC, signed in to your account, is drawn on the others.
  • One sync for Exo Cinema: your Exo account. The old mailbox sync is off. The first sign-in after this update sends what this PC holds to your account, merged by id, so nothing is counted twice; after that only the account syncs. Each show's audio and subtitle choice syncs too.
  • The account banner on Home. While this PC is signed out, a slim strip offers Create account; × hides it for a week, as Exo Launcher's does.
  • Your account keeps your watch history, resume points, watchlist, settings and add-ons in step. Signed in, Exo Cinema sends what changed to Exo's account service and takes what other PCs changed, in the account's own order: each watch is kept once, a settings choice you change on one PC lands on the others, and a removed add-on is removed everywhere. Settings › Account shows Sync, with its state and Sync now. Held items (too large, or refused by the service) say so. Glass or Solid and Reduce motion reach the account but not yet another PC's screen: only Play next episode is applied there.
  • The account step can't be skipped. Continue waits for a sign-in. If the account service can't be reached, Continue offline appears, with the same words Exo Launcher uses.
  • Signed out, a sync stops and waits. A session that ended says so under Sync, and the queue waits for your next sign-in.
  • Exo now has accounts, once. If you had Exo Cinema before accounts, one sheet offers one email and password for every Exo app, with the words Exo Launcher uses: Create account, Sign in or Not now. It is not offered again once you answer. A new PC's setup asks for the account right after Welcome.
  • Settings › Account reads as Exo Launcher's. The recovery code and delete forms ask for your password, Sign out asks you to confirm first, and the page ends with who is signed in. Delete says that your watch history, add-ons and settings stay on this PC.
  • This PC is one row in Signed-in PCs. Exo apps on one PC each keep their own device ID, so Cinema and Launcher on one PC show as one "This PC" with no Remove button, and the other PCs keep the service's order.
  • Setup sits on pure black in every appearance, and the switch knob has one shadow instead of two.
  • Help now opens getexo.app/help.
  • Settings is the same sheet in all three apps. Cinema's Settings has the sidebar of pages Exo Launcher and Exo Browser have, in the same 1160px sheet with 24px corners, a 20px title, 62px rows and one hairline, so moving between the three no longer changes how Settings looks or works. Account first, then Playback, Jellyfin, Add-ons, Storage and About, each drawn alone on its own page.
  • Your Exo account, one email and password for every Exo app. Settings › Account signs in to the same account Exo Launcher and Exo Browser use, or creates it. Signed in it shows the email, this PC's other sessions with Remove, Change password, a new recovery code and Delete my account. The password goes to the service once and is never kept; the session token is sealed with Windows DPAPI for this Windows user; the recovery code is shown once and kept nowhere. Cinema now reaches accounts.getexo.app through Egress, and PRIVACY.md says what the account holds.

1.4.11 — November 12, 2026

  • Stream links are opened over IPv6 first. A Jellyfin server that gets its links from an add-on has them made for the server's own address, and the add-on refuses them on an IPv4 connection and plays them on an IPv6 one. Which one a PC used was left to Windows, and VPN software often tells Windows to prefer IPv4 and leaves it so: the same account then played on one PC and gave "the stream host made this link for another connection" on another, on the same network. The look at a link and the player now connect over IPv6 whenever the host and the PC have it, and over IPv4 at once when IPv6 doesn't connect (IPv6 is then left alone for five minutes).

1.4.10 — November 12, 2026

  • When the server's links are made for another connection, your add-on is asked from this PC, as Stremio asks it. A Jellyfin server that gets its streams from an add-on can hand out links their host made for the server's connection, which the host then refuses on your PC. With your stream add-on's link in Settings › Add-ons, Exo Cinema now asks that add-on itself for the film or the episode (by its IMDb id, which the server gives), so the links are made for this PC, and plays the best one. Where you left off and the watched mark still go to the server. Without an add-on in Settings nothing changes.

1.4.9 — November 12, 2026

  • A Jellyfin server that gets its streams from an add-on plays on your PC. Such a server asks the add-on for each film's links itself, so the stream host makes them for the server's connection and refuses them on your PC's, for every version: "This stream link only works on the internet connection that requested it". 1.4.8 fixed this only for add-ons used directly. Now a version refused that way is played through the server, which the host does accept; the server passes such a file on in one piece, so it plays from its start and can't be sought in or resumed. When the server won't pass it on either, the next version is tried, and the notice is in plain words without the addresses the host printed.
  • The whole link is looked at before it plays. A link can pass through several hosts before the file (the server, an add-on, the add-on behind it, the file's host). Only the first was checked, so a refusal further on played as a notice video in place of the film. Up to six hops are now checked, the server's own address included, and a refused version gives way to the next.
  • A film none of whose versions the host accepts is reported. The report says how many versions were refused, at which hop and whether it was about the connection; it holds no address. _All three: checked in tests (which redirects are notices, the wording), and the hops of a real server's links were followed by hand from this PC (four hops, the file at the last). Not checked: a refused link, since none could be made from here, and a server that passes a remote file on._
  • Search answers sooner on a Jellyfin server. Search asked the server for whole items, which a large or remote library takes seconds to gather. It now asks the server's quick search, which returns just each match's name, year and pictures, and falls back to the old request on a server without it. A term's answer is kept for three minutes, so typing back to it is immediate. _Timed by hand against one remote server from this PC: 0.6 to 1.5 s for the quick search, 3 to 10 s for the request used before. The reading of its answer is checked in tests; not seen on screen._

1.4.8 — November 12, 2026

  • "This stream link only works on the internet connection that requested it" no longer sticks. Some stream hosts make a link for the connection that asked for it and refuse it anywhere else. Exo Cinema kept a title's list of links for as long as it stayed open, so after the connection changed (a VPN switched on or off, a new address from the provider, the PC back from sleep on another network) every Play of that title tried the same refused links, and opening the title again did not help. A list is now played from for ten minutes; when every link in a kept list is refused, the add-ons are asked again at once and the new links are tried; refused links are never kept. When the new links are refused too, the notice says what to do in plain words and no longer shows the addresses the host printed.
  • No crash report on closing. Closing the window could end Exo Cinema with an error twice over: the freeze watch asked a window that was already gone to answer, on its own thread, where the error ended the app; and the page watch's last tick read the closed window's state. Both now find the window gone and stop. _Seen seven times in one PC's crash log, each at a close. Checked in tests for the freeze watch; the page watch's change is checked in code only._
  • A request Exo Cinema gave up is not reported as your add-on failing. Leaving a title while an add-on was still answering no longer counts as an add-on failure.

1.4.7 — November 5, 2026

  • The anime upscaler's warm-up never competes with a film. Its first shader compile runs only while nothing plays: it pauses when a film starts and resumes when the film stops, so a film doesn't stall for it. A film started before the upscaler is ready plays without ArtCNN (the log says so), and the next film gets it. The warm-up now compiles 1080p clips, so a second start switches ArtCNN on at once with no compile, and a warm-up that draws no frame no longer counts as done (1.4.6 marked it done after none had).
  • The film's clock keeps time with the window unfocused. With the controls hidden (no pointer on the window for 2.5 seconds), the time and the seek bar froze at the point the controls went, while the film played on. They were drawn only in a frame loop that stopped with the controls. They are now painted four times a second while a film plays, and stop when it is paused, nothing is open or the window is minimized. Checked with tests/player-clock.test.mjs and a live test copy: a film on the second screen, unfocused, controls hidden, sampled every 5 seconds for 60 seconds, with the time following the clock (the live run used Cyberpunk, as Moana would not open that day).

1.4.6 — November 5, 2026

  • Crash, freeze and error reports, on by default. Exo Cinema sends a report to Exo's service when it crashes (at its next start), when its window stops answering for five seconds, when a film ends with a playback error, when the Jellyfin server doesn't answer three times in a row, and when one of your add-ons fails. Each holds the app's name, its version, the Windows build, the error's name and a few log lines, with every address, file path, file name, title, user and machine name, email and token taken out; the service checks it again. No install ID and no account; one report per kind of error a day and five a day at most; deleted after 30 days. Diagnostics in Settings › About says what's sent, and PRIVACY.md lists every field. There is no switch.
  • app.log keeps the last run. Each start begins a new app.log, and the run before is kept as app.prev.log; each file stops at 512 KB, keeping the newest lines. The log now also says when an add-on or a stream fails (the kind of failure and how long it took, never the address), when Egress refuses a connection (by its purpose), how a film ended in mpv, and when an update check doesn't answer.
  • Jellyfin says "Checking…" until it answers, not "Not answering". A slow answer no longer marks your server offline. A read that drops, resets or times out is tried once more, and Settings says "Not answering" only when both tries fail. While it's offline, Settings and Home ask again on a backoff (15 s, then longer), and Settings asks each time it opens. Jellyfin's rooms that were still on their way (Continue, Next up, favorites, Airing soon) fill in when they arrive. Airing soon's cards show only the season and episode the server gives. app.log gets one line per failure, its kind and how long it took, never the address.
  • Airing soon on Home. Signed in to Jellyfin, a row after Next up lists the episodes your server has coming: each show, its episode as S1 E2, and when it airs (Today, Tomorrow, the weekday, or the date, at its local time). Pressing one opens the show's page; an episode that hasn't aired can't be played. The row isn't drawn when the server has none or doesn't answer.
  • Another version when one won't open. A Jellyfin title whose version Play chose won't open before its first frame moves to the next of its versions, from the same place, up to five in all. A version you picked on the sheet stays as picked and says why it won't open. When no version is left, a title with just one says what its server listed, the version's name, not only "loading failed". Those names come through as plain text.
  • No freeze at the first anime upscale. The first shader compile (about a minute on some PCs) no longer holds the window: the window keeps answering while it runs, and the upscaler is switched in once it is ready. The freeze watch also reports a film page that stops answering for five seconds (as Hang.Page; the window's own freeze is Hang.Ui).
  • A crash on the UI thread reaches crash.log. A throw on the window's thread, in a posted callback, a page event or a timer, used to end Exo Cinema without a line in crash.log. Each of those now writes its crash first, so the report goes out at the next start.

1.4.5 — November 5, 2026

  • Jellyfin's top bar and buttons at every size. The bar and its buttons answer the first click at any window size, after Maximize and Restore too, not only at the size they opened at.
  • Cinema matches Exo Launcher. The player's controls and Settings use Launcher's buttons, switches, rows, words and hover fill, so the two look like one app.
  • What's New is the Changelog. The sheet opens the Changelog, with each release's notes, in place of the old What's New list.
  • Smarter buffering. A file starts loading ahead of what you watch, its read-ahead is sized from the memory free at that moment and is resized live while a network film plays, and a Jellyfin version is chosen for your measured link speed, direct play only.
  • Jellyfin sign-in replaces the imports. Settings' Import group and its first-run offer are gone; connecting your Jellyfin server once brings your library, progress and favorites. An import an older copy synced is ignored.
  • Jellyfin's first run links. The server field starts at https://jlly.cc, to type over with your own server. Under it, Create an account, Set up AIOStreams and Get PublicMetaDB open those sites in your browser; nothing is sent to a server until you press Connect.
  • HDR on for HDR films. A film with an HDR picture switches its screen to HDR before its first frame and back off after it, with no setting. A next HDR film keeps HDR on through the change, and a refused switch is tried again.

1.4.4 — November 5, 2026

  • Your Jellyfin server is your library. Connect in Settings › Jellyfin, or in the first run, with your server's address, username and password. Connect says connected only after the server took the sign-in and then answered as your account; otherwise it says which it was: the address can't be reached, it isn't a Jellyfin server, the username or password is wrong, or the server answered with an error. One sign-in lasts: only the server's token is kept, sealed on this PC, never the password. Home, Movies, Shows, search and title pages then fill from the server, its rows first and Cinemeta's gone; add-ons stay in Settings for anyone who wants them, and nothing asks for one.
  • HDR for films, by itself. A film or show with an HDR picture turns HDR on for its screen while it plays, when that screen can show HDR and has it off, and turns it back off after. When the next HDR episode starts within ten seconds, it stays on through the change, so it doesn't flicker. No setting; a screen already in HDR is never touched, and nothing switches while a game runs.
  • In step with your other Jellyfin apps. Continue Watching, Next Up, played and favorites are read from the server each time; what you play reports its place at its start, every 30 seconds, on a seek, on pause and at its stop; Watched and Watchlist write to the server. It stays out of Exo Watch. It plays the server's original file and never asks for a conversion. Another app sees this only as far as it reads and reports to the server too.

1.4.3 — November 5, 2026

  • Exo Launcher's look. Tabler's newest icons at a lighter line, Geist everywhere, subtitles included, and the top right button is now Settings' gear, with your profile inside Settings.

1.4.2 — October 31, 2026

Exo Cinema no longer needs a Jellyfin server: films and shows come from add-ons, with Continue Watching, the Watchlist and History built in.

  • Add-ons in place of Jellyfin. Home, Movies, Shows, search and every title's page come from Stremio-protocol add-ons. Cinemeta, Stremio's public catalog, is built in, so Home is full on a first start. Your add-ons go in Settings › Add-ons, in your order, and their rows come before Cinemeta's. Each shows its name and host, never the rest of its link. The one stream add-on from before moves into the list by itself. Jellyfin's sign-in, its sealed token and the server's address are gone from this PC and from your profile.
  • Exo Watch, built in. Continue Watching keeps where you stop every 30 seconds and when you stop, and adds each show's next episode after the last one you watched. The Watchlist and History are Exo Cinema's own, and a show's page opens on the episode to play next.
  • Movies and Shows. Each list (Popular, Featured, your add-ons' own) shows as a grid that grows as you scroll, narrowed by genre from the menu beside it.
  • Signed in, it follows you. With your Exo account, your add-ons go to your other PCs with Exo Watch, end-to-end encrypted, so a new PC has them once you sign in. Joining a profile adds this PC's add-ons to it.
  • Import from Jellyfin, Emby or Stremio. Settings › Import signs in once, brings what you watched (with the day), where you left off and your favorites or library into History, Continue Watching and the Watchlist, and signs out. A Stremio show's watched episodes come too, each one Stremio marked. Once an import brings something in, it leaves Settings on every PC. Jellyfin's Quick Connect works without typing a password. The Netflix import is gone.
  • Plays without waiting on a slow add-on. With Source on Best, a film or episode starts once a good stream is in: a stream your debrid service already holds gives the other add-ons 1.5 s more, any other stream 4 s, and nothing waits past 12 s. Ask still lists every add-on's streams.
  • Pictures through the app. Posters, backdrops, logos and episode stills come through Exo Cinema's own picture cache, each fetch through the same checkpoint as everything else it connects to. PRIVACY names Cinemeta's, MetaHub's and TMDB's picture hosts.
  • Up to date. libmpv's October 30 build (mpv v0.41.0-1372, FFmpeg N-128212). The Windows App SDK, the WebView2 SDK and .NET 10.0.14 were already the newest.
  • Files open straight away. A file opened with Exo Cinema starts playing while the rest of the app comes up; local files read 10 s ahead with a 64 MB cap instead of minutes. The anime upscaler's first compile waits until a non-anime video stops.
  • Quiet while you watch. Hidden player controls stop scheduling animation and video glass work.
  • Reliable player controls. Typing and IME input keep their keys; unusual track language tags no longer break the track menu.

1.4.0 — October 29, 2026

Surround to your receiver as it was mixed, picture choices made for the graphics card you have, and seeking that follows the video again.

  • Surround as it was mixed. With an AV receiver or soundbar on HDMI as Windows' sound output, Dolby Atmos (TrueHD and Dolby Digital Plus), DTS:X and the rest now go to it untouched, so it plays their height channels; before, they reached it flattened to 7.1. Exo Cinema asks Windows which formats your output takes and sends only those, and the receiver's own volume sets the level, so the volume slider steps aside meanwhile. It stays off while another app plays through the same output, while a game runs and during an Exo voice call, and a call or a game starting mid-film gets the sound back in two seconds. Headphones, speakers and USB or Bluetooth sound play as before.
  • The graphics card you have. The player now asks Direct3D about the card it draws on, instead of going by its name: whether it's built in, and which video formats it decodes itself. On a laptop with two graphics chips it draws on the faster one while plugged in, so its GeForce or Radeon gets High quality and the upscalers. Intel's built-in Arc graphics and AMD's built-in RX Vega were taken for graphics cards, and a Radeon PRO for built-in graphics; each now gets what it can run. Best picks a stream above 1080p only when your card decodes it itself, so a card without AV1 or HEVC decoding no longer starts a 4K stream it would decode in software. A choice made in Windows' Settings › Graphics still wins.
  • Seeking follows the video again. In 1.3.0, once the anime upscaler had been built in the background, the seek bar and the time could stop following the video for as long as it had already played. They follow it again, and the hover pictures let go of their connection before a jump. A stream whose host can't jump within it is passed over for the next one that can; when none can, a jump goes as far as what's loaded.
  • Updates from Exo's own copy. New versions download from Exo's copy of each release on Cloudflare; GitHub sends one only in the minutes before its copy is there. PRIVACY says so.
  • Up to date. libmpv's October 29 build (mpv v0.41.0-1349, FFmpeg N-128186); the Windows App SDK, the WebView2 SDK and .NET 10.0.14 were already the newest.

1.3.0 — October 28, 2026

Pictures on the seek bar, scrubbing that keeps up, the best stream and tracks picked by their details, and anime redrawn by ArtCNN.

  • See where you'd jump. Hover the seek bar and a picture of that moment shows above it, with the time: your Jellyfin server's own previews when it made them (trickplay), otherwise drawn on the spot from the nearest keyframe by a second player Exo Cinema keeps hidden, in about 15 ms a picture. It closes a minute after you stop hovering, and when you leave the player. Not for a stream the server converts, where every picture would start another conversion.
  • Scrubbing that keeps up. Dragging fast left the time and its glass trailing the pointer, and seeks sent in a row could make the bar jump back to an earlier one. The time and the picture now move with the pointer at the screen's own pace, and the bar holds where you sent it until the video lands there.
  • Ask lists every stream. With Settings › Playback › Source on Ask, a title from a stream add-on now lists the add-on's streams, best first, each with what its name says: 4K, Dolby Vision, HEVC, remux or web, size, bitrate and sound. It used to play without asking, since the server holds such a title once.
  • The best stream, picked by its details. On Best, Exo Cinema ranks what your add-on returns, after its own filters and sort. First comes a stream that can start now (the debrid service already holds it), never a camera copy, and one your connection has carried smoothly before. Then come the most detail your PC is built for, a picture your graphics card decodes, the cleanest source, HDR, the fullest sound and the most seeders, and the add-on's own order last.
  • The right audio and subtitles. A video starts on the best recording in the language you'd expect: lossless before lossy, more channels before fewer, never a commentary track, and Japanese for anime. When the audio isn't in your language you get full subtitles in yours (styled ones first, as anime's are, never signs only); for a dub, only its signs and songs. What you pick yourself still wins, for the rest of the show.
  • Anime, sharper. Anime is redrawn by ArtCNN, a neural network trained on drawn art: lines come out crisp and backgrounds keep their texture. 720p, 1080p and 1440p episodes are drawn at up to 4K and shrunk back to your screen. Exo Cinema builds ArtCNN for your graphics card once, in the background (about 40 seconds, never while a game runs), and uses the driver's upscaler until then. Chosen on a picture bench against Anime4K and NVIDIA's upscaler, where it came closest to the clean original on every test.
  • Sharper pictures. High quality now shrinks a bigger picture with Catmull-Rom instead of mpv's softer default. NVIDIA's RTX Video Super Resolution now only enlarges a video smaller than your screen: drawn at twice the size and shrunk back, a full-size picture came out softer than without it.
  • Starts sooner. The player keeps the shaders it builds for your graphics card instead of building them again at every start.
  • Exo's own subtitles. Plain subtitles (SRT, WebVTT) are drawn in Inter, Exo's type, with a dark edge and a soft shadow; styled ones keep their own look and fonts.
  • The poster in Windows. Windows' media controls and Exo Launcher's mini player now show the poster of what's playing (the show's, for an episode). It was missing.
  • Full screen stays put. Going to or from full screen never makes Exo Cinema the active window, in front of your game or behind it, and a full-screen window can't be dragged or snapped by its top edge.
  • Glass that fits. A title that changes length, or a menu filling in, resizes its glass at once.
  • Reduce motion from the start. The first run's Look step has the Reduce motion switch too, and Setup's own animations play again.
  • Installed apps. Windows' Installed apps lists Exo as the publisher, with links to getexo.app and the Exo Discord, and Exo Cinema keeps its version there right.
  • Up to date. Every part was already the newest: libmpv's October 28 build, .NET 10.0.14, the Windows App SDK and the WebView2 SDK. ArtCNN (MIT) joins the third-party notices.

1.2.0 — October 28, 2026

Updates offered as Exo Cinema opens, your add-on link back in reach, a Reduce motion switch of its own, and a window that stays behind your game.

  • Updates when you open it. Exo Cinema looks for a new version once, as it opens, and offers it on a sheet: "Update available", the version and Update now, which downloads it with its progress, checks it and installs it; the new version opens and What's New shows what changed. A version that comes out while Exo Cinema is open waits for the next open, so nothing interrupts a film; the twelve-hour check is gone. A failed update says why, with Copy logs, Try again and Not now; one that didn't take is offered with Not now from the start, so nobody is held in a loop. The check asks Exo's update address (updates.getexo.app) first and GitHub when it doesn't answer, so a renamed account can't strand an installed Exo Cinema.
  • Sheets hold the window. While What's New or the update sheet is up, nothing behind it scrolls or reacts to keys, and a press on the dim does nothing; the window's own buttons stay usable on top.
  • Your add-on link, when you need it. Settings › Playback › Stream addon showed a saved link as dots even to you, with no way to read or copy it. It now shows in full while you're in the field and as dots otherwise (so it stays off screenshots and streams), and Copy copies it without showing it.
  • Reduce motion, your way. Settings › Look has a Reduce motion switch, as Exo Launcher has. The first start takes it from Windows' animation effects; after that only the switch decides, and it always shows what's in effect. Windows' setting no longer quietly stills Exo Cinema with the switch Off.
  • Stays behind your game. Exo Cinema came to the front by itself in two ways: a sheet focusing itself as a window opened in the background, and a change to or from full screen (an episode ending while you play) with another window in use. Focus the page moves by itself now waits for you to click into Exo Cinema, and a full-screen change waits until it's the active window.
  • Up to date. libmpv is zhongfly's LGPL build of October 28 (mpv v0.41.0-1346-gdc4b285c9, FFmpeg N-128155-g8e362b7b5); every other part was already the newest.

1.1.0 — October 28, 2026

RTX upscaling without the ripple, an app that rests when you're not using it, pins that survive updates, and Exo Launcher's notices and motion.

  • RTX upscaling without the ripple. RTX Video Super Resolution was sized from the screen's height, not the window's: in a maximized window (shorter than the screen) or for a widescreen film (1920x800 on a 1080p screen went up to 2592x1080), mpv then shrank the upscaled picture back by a sliver, and that made the whole video ripple and look wavy. It now upscales to exactly the size the video is shown at, so mpv doesn't scale it again, and sizes itself again when the window is resized or goes full screen.
  • Next to nothing in the background. The player's progress bar redrew on every frame forever, even with no video open: about 6% of a CPU core at idle, minimized or behind a game. It now draws only while it's on screen and moving, and moves on transforms instead of relaying the page out; the Skip and Up Next check runs only while a video is open; a minimized Exo Cinema stops drawing its page and gives memory back; and its spinners pause while another window is in front.
  • Pins that stay. Windows ties a Start or taskbar pin to an app's ID. 1.0.8 put Exo Cinema's own ID on its shortcuts, which changed it and unpinned Exo Cinema; Setup now writes that ID on the shortcuts as it makes them, so it never changes again. If 1.0.8 unpinned it, pin it once more and it stays.
  • Notices like Exo Launcher's. Messages show at the top right under the bar, stack, stay 4.5 seconds and wait while the pointer is on them.
  • Motion like Exo Launcher's. Every time and curve now comes from Exo Launcher's tokens: the boot, fades and the progress fill were snapped to them; menus grow from their button and sheets settle in; the light a press makes on glass is drawn once and only fades and grows (it repainted the glass on every frame of a press); and under Windows' reduce motion nothing loops.
  • Up to date. The Windows SDK build tools are on 10.0.28000.2957; .NET 10.0.14, libmpv (mpv v0.41.0-1320) and every other package were already the newest.

1.0.8 — October 28, 2026

Exo Cinema joins Windows' media controls.

  • Media keys and Windows' media controls. While the player is open, Exo Cinema is a media session in Windows: the keyboard's play, pause and next keys work with another window in front, and Windows' media controls show the title, the show and episode (or the year) and the poster, with play and pause, a timeline to seek on, 10 seconds back and forward, and Next when a next episode follows. Closing the player ends the session, so the media keys go back to your music.
  • In Exo Launcher's Now Playing. Exo Launcher reads Windows' media sessions, so it can show and steer Exo Cinema like its other sources (app ID UhhErix.ExoCinema).
  • Its own name and icon. Exo Cinema stamps its app ID on its own Start menu and desktop shortcuts, so Windows names it "Exo Cinema" with its icon in the media controls, and a pinned taskbar icon stays one icon.
  • Opens during a driver update. While a graphics driver installs, its Vulkan loader is gone for a minute or two and mpv can't load; Exo Cinema opened to an empty window then. Now it opens as usual, and the player starts the next time it's needed (or Play says to try again once the driver is done).

Your links and keys stay yours, on screen and in the log.

1.0.5 — October 28, 2026

Settings with one right answer are now just done.

  • The picture picks itself. Settings > Playback > Picture is gone. Exo Cinema draws High quality (mpv's high-quality scalers, with RTX Video Super Resolution where it's on) on a dedicated graphics card on mains power, and Balanced on integrated graphics and on battery. A video that drops a frame a second for a minute goes Balanced for the rest of it; the next one starts on High again. The player's readings (I) say which.
  • Hardware decoding, always. Its switch is gone: video is decoded on the graphics card, and mpv falls back to software for anything the card can't decode, as it did with the switch on.
  • First run: the Playback step now sets Source and Play next episode.

1.0.4 — October 28, 2026

AI upscaling on RTX cards, and updates that wait for the folder.

  • AI upscaling on RTX cards. A video smaller than the screen (720p on a 1080p screen, 1080p on a 1440p or 4K one) is enlarged by NVIDIA's RTX Video Super Resolution as it plays, through mpv's Direct3D 11 video processor, as Edge and Chrome ask for it: sharper edges and finer detail than any scaler can draw. It takes Picture on High quality, SDR video decoded on the card, and RTX Video Super Resolution on in Exo Launcher's PC > Display (or NVIDIA's own settings); Exo Cinema reads that switch and never changes it. With it off, mpv's own high-quality scalers enlarge the picture as before.
  • Games come first. While Exo Launcher has a game running, the upscaler steps aside so the game keeps the graphics card, and comes back when the game ends.
  • Updates that wait. An update stopped at "Exo Cinema's folder is in use" when one of the app's WebView2 helpers was still closing; Setup now tries again for up to 20 seconds. Found by Exo Browser, whose updates failed the same way.

1.0.3 — October 28, 2026

The best version of every title, or your pick.

  • The best version, every time. A title your server has in more than one version (4K and 1080p, a remux and a smaller copy) played whichever the server listed first. Play now takes the best one for this PC: one it can play as it is before one the server would convert, then the sharpest picture this PC is built for (above 1080p only on a dedicated graphics card), then the highest bitrate. Up Next and autoplay take it too.
  • Or choose it yourself. Settings > Playback > Source on Ask shows every version when you press Play: its picture (4K, Dolby Vision, HEVC), its name, size, bitrate and sound, the best one marked; Escape or Cancel puts it away, and a title with one version plays without asking.

1.0.2 — October 27, 2026

Skip where you look, rows that page, and no white boxes.

  • Skip where you look. Skip intro, Skip credits and Up Next sat at the right end of the control bar, near the middle of a wide screen; they sit at the right of the screen now, higher and clear of the controls, and Skip is larger (56 px, 16 px words, up from 44 and 13).
  • Rows that page. Every row has Exo Launcher's Store arrows beside its name: each moves the row nine tenths of its width, one turns off at its end, and a row that fits shows none.
  • Pictures that lift. Pointing at a poster, a wide card or an episode lifts its picture 3 px toward you and lights it a little, as Exo Launcher's covers do; nothing grows, and Windows' reduce motion keeps it still. Rows have room above for the lift.
  • No white boxes. Glass ringed whatever had the focus inside it, so the search field wore a white box as you typed, and a field clicked into, or a button clicked and then a key pressed (Space to pause), wore Chromium's ring. Rings now show only while you move with Tab, and a click puts them away.
  • Play starts playing. The player opens on the first click of Play or Resume, so a second click, or a double click, landed on the picture and paused the video (or went full screen) before it began. The picture takes no clicks while a video opens.
  • How playback went, in the log. While a video plays, app.log gets a line a minute with the frames dropped and late in that minute, the decoder, the size, the format and the frame rate.

1.0.1 — October 27, 2026

Search that answers and says it's looking.

  • Search answers sooner. Each search asked the server for every detail a title's page uses, the episode counts of every show among them, and for a count of every match; on a server with a 10,000-title library that took up to 13 seconds. It now asks only for what the list shows: about a second for a common word, a third of a second once the server has seen it. A word the server hasn't searched lately can still take it several seconds on a library that size.
  • Search shows it's looking. A spinner turns in the field while the server looks, a search you've typed past is called off so the server works only on the latest, and the list says No matches, or why the search failed, instead of staying empty.
  • No ring on What's New. It opened with Continue focused, and with nothing pressed yet the page drew its white keyboard ring around the button; the sheet takes the focus now, and Enter or Escape still continue.

1.0.0 — October 27, 2026

The first release: your Jellyfin library in Exo's look, with a native player.

  • Your Jellyfin library. Sign in with your server's address, user name and password, or with Quick Connect where the server offers it. Home shows Continue Watching, Next Up and what's new in each library; the Bar holds your libraries; a show's page lists its seasons and episodes; Ctrl+K searches the whole server.
  • Progress that follows you. Where you stop, pauses and what you finish go back to Jellyfin as you watch, so every Jellyfin app picks up where you left off, and Watched on a title's page marks it on the server.
  • A native player. libmpv draws the picture under the interface, with hardware decoding, HDR and Dolby Vision tone mapped for the screen, and mpv's high-quality scaling on a dedicated graphics card (Settings > Playback > Picture picks Balanced for lighter scaling).
  • Liquid Glass over the picture. The controls blur and dim the video under them as it plays, drawn by the player on the graphics card, so the glass moves with every frame and costs playback nothing.
  • Audio, subtitles and speed from one menu at the top right, under its button. Each show remembers the audio and subtitle languages you pick, and the subtitles rise above the controls while they show.
  • Skip intro and Up Next. Skip openings and credits from Jellyfin's media segments, or the file's own chapter names; near the end of an episode the next one counts down and plays unless you choose Not now (Settings > Playback > Play next episode turns that off).
  • Steady over a slow link. The player reads ahead as far as memory allows (up to 1 GB, by how much the PC has), waits for 3 seconds of picture before playing on after it runs dry, and reconnects when a server drops the stream.
